# Visual styling

The scene is a fixed, side-on 2D view of a European settlement on a rainy autumn day. Roofs step up and down with the terrain. Trees interrupt the rows of buildings, and the street gives the eye a path through the image.

## Watercolor and autumn color

Paint the landscape in translucent washes with irregular pigment edges and a fine paper grain. Give nearby branches, rooflines, and window frames a few defined marks. Let larger surfaces blend softly where one wash meets another. Hold the paper grain steady as the painted elements move.

Mix rust reds, burnt oranges, wet browns, and muted greens through the trees. A hillside can carry groups of similar color, with a few green trees among the turning ones. Wet stone, timber, and slate sit in cooler washes. Leaves gather along curbs and park paths, and an occasional warm window gives the eye a point of focus.

## Fog and depth

Fog is the main way the scene conveys depth. Give each view at least three readable depths.

| Depth | Treatment |
| --- | --- |
| Foreground | Nearby trunks, branches, and curbs have the clearest edges and strongest color. Low wisps can pass through them. |
| Middle distance | Buildings, gardens, and trees define the street. Bands of fog move between them, veiling a façade while a closer trunk stays visible. |
| Distance | Hills and distant roofs soften into pale washes. Their outlines emerge and disappear as fog crosses the slope. |

Keep banks of fog at several depths and let them drift at slightly different speeds. Let low fog collect in hollows, flow between buildings, and partly cover doorways. A good depth cue is a near roof that stays clear as another roof disappears into the same bank behind it.

## Movement and reveals

Use fine rain, quiet puddle ripples, and the occasional drip from an eave. Keep the motion gentle enough to watch for a while.

For regeneration, send a denser bank over the affected area. It can wrap around a building site, flow along a street, or spread across the view. The bank needs to cover the full extent of the change, including where a new roofline might extend beyond the old one. Roll it out gradually to reveal the new shapes and colors.
